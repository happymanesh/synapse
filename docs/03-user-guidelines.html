Part A — Everyday Use

1. Signing In

  1. Open Synapse using the address provided by your administrator.
  2. Enter your username and password and select Sign in.
  3. If a message appears when you sign in, read it and select Close. Messages appear once; you can find them again from the notification bell.

Password rules

Your password must have at least eight characters and include at least one uppercase letter, one number, and one special character from ! @ # $ % ^ & *. It cannot be the same as your username, and it cannot repeat a password you have used recently.

If you cannot sign in

SituationWhat to do
You have forgotten your user IDSelect the help link on the sign-in page and submit a request. An administrator will contact you.
You have forgotten your passwordSubmit a password reset request from the same help link. Passwords cannot be recovered, only reset.
Your credentials are correct but sign-in is refusedYour account may be restricted to particular office networks, or it may be inactive. Contact your administrator.
Sessions expire. After a period of inactivity you will be returned to the sign-in page. This is normal; sign in again to continue.

The menu panel

The menu runs down the left side in up to three levels: main menu, sub-menu and menu option. Opening one main menu closes any other, so only one section is expanded at a time. The option you are currently viewing is marked with a green dot.

The « button at the top collapses the menu to a narrow strip of icons, giving your report more room. Selecting an icon in that strip opens its contents beside it. Select » to restore the full menu.

The working area

Everything to the right of the menu. When you move between screens, the top bar and menu stay in place and only this area changes — you can keep using the menu while a screen loads.

3. Switching Between Applications

Synapse hosts several applications. You see only those you are entitled to use.

  1. Select the grid of dots in the top-right corner.
  2. Choose an application. The one you are in is outlined.
  3. The menu changes to that application's structure, and its name appears in the top bar.
Synapse remembers. The application you were last using reopens automatically the next time you sign in. Search, favourites and frequently used options always relate to the application you are currently in.

If you have turned on New window in the menu panel, choosing an application opens it in a new browser tab instead of replacing the current one.

4. Finding What You Need

Search

Type into the search field in the top bar. It matches both the name of a menu option and its position, so searching for Sample finds everything beneath a Sample sub-menu. Each result shows its full path. Select a result to go there.

Favourites

Open any screen and select the heart in the top bar to add it to your favourites. The heart fills in to confirm. Your favourites appear as cards at the top of the dashboard. Select the heart again to remove it.

Frequently used options

Beneath Favourites, the dashboard shows the five options you have opened most often, with a count on each card. This list builds itself from your activity over the last seven days — there is nothing to configure.

5. Running a Report

  1. Open the report from the menu. The path to it appears at the top of the screen.
  2. Fill in the parameters. Fields marked with a red asterisk are required. Some dropdowns become available only after you have chosen a value above them — for example a product list that depends on the company you selected.
  3. Select Show. The parameters collapse automatically to give the results room.

Working with results

To do thisDo this
Sort by a columnSelect the column heading. Select again to reverse. Dates and numbers sort by value, not by how they read.
Narrow the rows shownSelect Column filter and type into the filter row beneath the headings.
See the parameters againSelect Show Parameters at the top right of the breadcrumb line.
Hide the parametersSelect Hide Parameters in the same place.
Start overSelect Reset to clear all parameters.
ExportSelect Download and choose CSV. The file is named with your username and the date and time.

Reading the display

  • Dates appear as 26-Jul-2026 throughout, so there is never any doubt about day and month order.
  • Numbers are right-aligned; amounts show their configured decimal places.
  • Some reports total a column beneath the last row.
  • Some reports colour rows automatically to draw attention to particular conditions.
  • A note beneath the table may explain how the figures should be used.
"Too many rows to display." If your selection returns more rows than the report is allowed to show on screen, Synapse offers a download instead. Either download the file, or narrow your parameters — a shorter date range is usually the quickest way.

6. Drilling Into Detail

In some reports, values appear as links. Selecting one opens a related report showing the detail behind that figure, carrying your current parameters across automatically.

  • If the detail opens as a full page, use ← Back to … at the top left to return. Your original report reappears exactly as you left it, parameters and results intact.
  • If the detail opens as a popup, select Close.

The detail report does not show its own parameter panel, because its values were decided by the figure you selected.

7. Adding and Editing Records

Some menu options are data-entry screens rather than reports. They look similar, but the button reads Save and your saved records are listed underneath.

  1. Complete the fields and select Save.
  2. To change a record, select Edit on its row. The form fills with its values and the button changes to Update.
  3. To remove a record, select Delete and confirm.
You see only your own records. The list beneath the form shows records you created. Other users' entries are never shown to you, and yours are not shown to them. Reports built on the same data may still show everything — that is intentional and separate.

8. Notifications

The bell in the top bar carries a red badge showing unread messages.

  • Select the bell to see your messages with the date and time each was sent.
  • Opening the list marks those messages as read and clears the badge. Messages stay in the list.
  • Some messages also appear as a popup when you sign in. Select Close to dismiss it; it will not appear again.

Part B — Administration

This part applies only if you hold an administrator role. Administration appears in the menu regardless of which application you are working in.

10. Administration

10.1 Order of setup

Each step depends on the ones before it.

  1. Company — the legal entity everything else belongs to.
  2. App — the applications within that company.
  3. Hierarchy and Client Category — organisational positions and client classifications.
  4. Menu — the navigation structure, with each menu assigned to an application.
  5. Role — named permission sets, then assign menus to each.
  6. User — the people, then assign roles to each.

Common to every administration screen

ControlPurpose
Search boxFilters the list as you type
Filter by companyWhere records belong to a company
ExportDownloads the list as it is currently filtered
+ AddCreates a new record
✏️ / 🗑️Edit and delete
Fields appear as they become relevant. Choosing a menu type of Report reveals the report selector and hides the route and external address fields. This is expected — only the fields that apply to your selection are shown.

10.2 Company and App

Company holds the legal entity details and the logo shown in the top bar.

App defines each application: a code, a name, the company it belongs to, an emoji icon or a logo path, and a display order controlling its position in the switcher.

You do not grant access to an application directly. A user can open an application if any menu they are entitled to belongs to it. Assign the menu, and the application follows.

10.3 Hierarchy and Client Category

Hierarchy lists organisational positions, each with a sequence controlling display order. Client Category classifies clients; C00 is the default for new users.

10.4 Users and access

  1. Create the user in User Master with their company, hierarchy and category. The initial password must satisfy the password policy.
  2. Assign roles using 🛡️ on the user's row, and tick the roles that apply.
  3. Restrict by network if required in User IP Mapping. Patterns may use wildcards, such as 192.168.*.*.
A user with no IP mapping can sign in from anywhere. Adding one restricts them to matching addresses. Add entries only when a restriction is genuinely intended.

Use 🔑 to reset a password. Deactivating a user prevents sign-in while preserving their history — prefer this to deletion.

10.6 Building a report

  1. Filter Components — create each input once. Choose its type, and for a dropdown either a fixed list or a query returning value and label.
  2. Filters — create a filter, then use 📑 to arrange components into rows and positions, marking any that are mandatory.
  3. Reports — create the report with mode REPORT, choose the filter, and write the query using :COMPONENT_CODE where each value belongs. Set the maximum rows.
  4. Columns — use 📋 to define each column: label, order and data type. Set decimal places for amounts, and tick Show total where a footer total is wanted.
  5. Highlight rules — optionally use 🎨 to colour rows by condition.
  6. Publish — add a Menu of type Report pointing at it, assign it to an application, and grant it to the relevant roles.
Reuse component codes deliberately. When a summary report and its detail report use the same component for something like Company or Date Range, values carry across automatically when a user drills down. Creating separate components with different codes breaks that.

10.7 Building a data-entry form

Follow the same steps with these differences:

  • Set the report mode to FORM and name the Target table.
  • Give every filter item a Mapped column — this is what connects a field to the column it writes.
  • Mark the primary key column as the Identifier column so edit and delete know which record to act on.
  • The target table must have created_by, updated_by and updated_on columns, and the list query must include created_by.
Existing rows will not appear. Users see only records they created. Data loaded before the form existed has no owner and will not be listed — which is usually what you want, but it surprises people the first time.

10.8 Sending notifications

  1. Choose the audience: a whole hierarchy, or one named individual.
  2. Choose delivery: Popup shows the message once at next sign-in; Notification lists it under the bell. You may choose either or both.
  3. Write the message.
  4. Optionally schedule it. Leave the date and time blank to send immediately. A scheduled message is simply invisible to recipients until its time arrives — there is nothing further to do.

11. Troubleshooting

SymptomLikely cause and remedy
A menu option is missingEither it is not granted to any of your roles, or it belongs to a different application. Check the application switcher first.
An application is missing from the switcherYou have no menu belonging to it. An administrator must grant a role containing one of its menus.
Show does nothingA required parameter is empty. Required fields carry a red asterisk; a message appears beneath the parameters.
A dropdown is empty or disabledIt depends on a field above it. Choose that value first.
"The end date must be after the start date"Correct the range. The start date also cannot be in the future.
The report offers a download instead of resultsThe result is larger than the report may display. Download it, or narrow the date range.
Your saved records are not listedThe list shows only records you created. Records created by others, or loaded directly into the table, will not appear.
Returned to the sign-in page unexpectedlyYour session expired. Sign in again.
Favourites or frequently used look wrongBoth reflect the application you are currently in. Check the name in the top bar.
