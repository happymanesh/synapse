1. Purpose and Scope

1.1 Purpose

SIHL currently handles issues and service requests informally — phone calls into RMS, ad-hoc WhatsApp threads, undocumented email chases — with no single record of what was raised, by whom, or how long it took to resolve. This has already produced avoidable outcomes, including a client penalty caused by a verbal instruction being misheard over a phone call to RMS.

This module gives SIHL a single ticketing and service-request system, embedded in Synapse, covering every channel through which an issue can be raised, with a complete audit trail from intake to resolution.

1.2 In scope — Phase 1

  • Ticket intake across Website, WhatsApp, Phone and the Synapse application itself.
  • Automatic identification of the raiser against existing Synapse master data, with a fallback path for people not yet on record.
  • A predefined, business-owned issue taxonomy used for both routing and reporting.
  • Detection and merge of duplicate tickets, confirmed by a human before anything is combined.
  • A resolution workflow with forwarding, reminders and a complete audit trail.
  • Conversion of larger requirements into change requests, gated by management approval.
  • Automated delivery of routine documents (ledgers, margin reports, contract notes) as a lighter-weight service-request flow.
  • A basic operational stats report.

1.3 Out of scope / explicitly deferred

  • Management dashboard (CEO Command Center integration) — Phase 2.
  • Automated email intake via Google APIs — Phase 3.
  • Solutions repository and after-hours chatbot — Phase 3.
  • OTP/MFA on document requests — considered and explicitly not included; email/mobile match against master data is sufficient for Phase 1.
  • Severity-tiered response SLAs — considered and explicitly not included; Phase 1 uses one flat response target for every ticket.

2. Business Context

SIHL's support load spans internal employees, B2B customers, associate partners, clients and prospects, arriving through whichever channel is most convenient for the person raising the issue. Without a shared system of record, three problems recur:

  • No accountability trail. When an issue is handled by phone or informal chat, there is no record of who decided what, which becomes a real liability when a decision turns out to be wrong.
  • Duplicated effort. The same issue is often raised more than once across different channels, with no mechanism to recognise and merge it.
  • Manual, repetitive service work. Routine document requests (ledgers, margin reports, contract notes) consume staff time that a simple automated flow can remove entirely.

This module addresses all three by giving every issue a single, trackable record from the moment it is raised, and by automating the service requests that do not need human judgement at all.

5. Functional Requirements — Phase 1

5.1 Ticket intake

RefRequirementPriority
FR-1.1Tickets may be raised via Website (using predefined subject lines mapped to prescribed reasons), WhatsApp, Phone (logged by staff on the caller's behalf), and the Synapse application's own ticket-raise screen.Must
FR-1.2Email intake is not supported in Phase 1; automated email reading depends on the Google API integration deferred to Phase 3.Must
FR-1.3Every ticket records: raiser identity (or guest details), channel, category, description, timestamp and initial status.Must

5.2 Identity resolution

RefRequirementPriority
FR-2.1On ticket creation, the system resolves the raiser against existing Synapse master data by email or mobile number.Must
FR-2.2If no match is found, the system creates a guest ticket, capturing name and contact details as given, without blocking ticket creation.Must
FR-2.3Guest tickets may be reconciled to a master data record later, for example once a prospect is onboarded as a client.Should
FR-2.4On creation, an automated registration notification (email or SMS) is sent confirming the ticket number and expected response window.Must

5.3 Categorisation

RefRequirementPriority
FR-3.1A predefined issue taxonomy, structured as Product/Module × Issue Type, is used both to route tickets and as the reporting dimension.Must
FR-3.2The taxonomy is owned and revised by the support lead / desk head as new issue patterns emerge.Must
FR-3.3Category is selected at the point of intake, by the raiser on Website/Synapse or by staff when logging a Phone/WhatsApp ticket.Must

5.4 Duplicate detection and merge

RefRequirementPriority
FR-4.1While a staff member is answering any ticket, the system surfaces other open tickets as likely duplicates using structured matching: same category, same client/entity, and raised within a rolling 24–48 hour window.Must
FR-4.2Matching is deterministic on structured fields in Phase 1; free-text similarity matching is not included.Must
FR-4.3Staff must manually confirm a duplicate via a checkbox before any merge takes place — the system never merges automatically.Must
FR-4.4Confirmed duplicates are combined into a single thread, carrying forward both tickets' history.Must
Note. The combined thread's turnaround-time clock uses the earlier of the two original raised timestamps, so merging cannot be used to understate elapsed time in the average-turnaround metric (§8, FR-8.1). Pending final sign-off.

5.5 Resolution lifecycle

RefRequirementPriority
FR-5.1Support desk staff resolve a ticket directly or forward it to another team member.Must
FR-5.2Every forward is logged with who forwarded it, to whom, when and any accompanying remark, preserving a complete audit trail.Must
FR-5.3Staff receive a daily morning reminder listing their pending tickets and burn-down status.Must
FR-5.4Staff must update status and remarks on each ticket — marking it solved or providing a revised timeline.Must
FR-5.5The initial response target is a flat 12–24 hours for every ticket, regardless of category. Severity-based SLA tiers are explicitly out of scope for Phase 1.Must

5.6 Change request conversion

RefRequirementPriority
FR-6.1An issue identified as a larger development requirement, rather than a fixable bug, may be converted into a change request.Must
FR-6.2Conversion requires approval from Product/Ops management; the support desk cannot park an item in the backlog unilaterally.Must
FR-6.3On conversion, the originating ticket is closed with a notification that it is being considered for a future release, and the item is parked as an enhancement in the application backlog.Must

5.7 Automated service requests

RefRequirementPriority
FR-7.1A lighter-weight flow, separate from the general ticket, handles automated document delivery: ledgers, margin reports and contract notes.Must
FR-7.2Ledger requests require a mandatory client code.Must
FR-7.3Verification for document delivery is by email/mobile match against master data; no additional OTP/MFA step is required in Phase 1.Must
FR-7.4The generated document is emailed directly to the client's registered email address.Must
FR-7.5If an employee raises the request on a client's behalf, the client is copied on the delivery for transparency.Must

5.8 Reporting

RefRequirementPriority
FR-8.1A stats report tracks daily ticket closures, new tickets raised, tickets forwarded and average turnaround time.Must
FR-8.2No dashboard UI is included in Phase 1 — the CEO Command Center integration is Phase 2 (§12).Must

6. Business Rules

These govern behaviour that is not self-evident from the requirements above and must be preserved in any future change.

RefRule
BR-1A ticket must never fail to be created because the raiser cannot be identified — the guest ticket path is the fallback, not an error state.
BR-2Tickets are never merged automatically; a human must confirm every merge.
BR-3Change requests can only be created through management approval — never unilaterally by the support desk.
BR-4Document service requests deliver only to the client's registered email address on file, never to an address supplied in the request itself.
BR-5The issue-category taxonomy has one accountable owner (the support lead / desk head) at all times.

7. Non-Functional Requirements

RefRequirement
NFR-1Ticketing data resides in the existing Synapse PostgreSQL database — no new datastore is introduced.
NFR-2Every status change and forward is attributable to a specific user and timestamp, supporting both operational audit and any future dispute resolution.

NFR-4Identity resolution and guest-ticket fallback must not introduce a blocking failure mode on ticket creation.

9. Assumptions, Dependencies and Constraints

9.1 Assumptions

  • Employees and clients already exist in Synapse master data (the existing userdetails / hierarchy tables), which identity resolution can query directly.
  • Associate partners and prospects are generally not present in master data, which is why the guest ticket path exists.
  • The support lead / desk head is available and willing to own the issue-category taxonomy on an ongoing basis.

9.2 Dependencies

  • This module depends on Synapse's existing authentication, session and master-data model — it is built as a module inside Synapse, not a standalone system.

9.3 Constraints

  • Google API email intake and the after-hours bot are out of scope until Phase 3, and the bot specifically depends on a populated, curated solutions repository — it cannot be brought forward ahead of that content existing.

12. Glossary

TermMeaning
TicketA single tracked record of an issue, from intake through resolution.
Guest ticketA ticket raised by someone not found in Synapse master data; captured with name and contact only, reconciled later.
Service requestA lighter-weight, automatable request — currently scoped to document delivery — distinct from a general support ticket.
Change requestA ticket reclassified as a development requirement and approved by management for the backlog, rather than resolved as a normal issue.
Solutions repositoryA curated, human-approved set of past resolutions used to power the Phase 3 after-hours bot.
Master dataSynapse's existing userdetails / hierarchy tables, used to identify ticket raisers automatically.
